- ` 44.9s` In a solid, particles are packed tightly in fixed positions.
- ` 48.7s` But they're not stationary.
- ` 50.6s` Each one vibrates on the spot, all the time.
- ` 53.7s` In a liquid, particles are still close together, but they can slide past each other.
- ` 58.5s` That's why liquids flow.
- ` 60.6s` In a gas, particles are far apart.
- ` 63.1s` They move fast, in straight lines, until they hit something.
- ` 66.8s` And the space between gas particles?
- ` 69.3s` It's empty.
- ` 70.5s` Not air.
- ` 71.7s` Nothing at all.
- ` 73.2s` The particles are the air.

- ` 76.7s` Now let's heat a gas.

- ` 95.9s` Notice what doesn't change: the size of each particle.
- ` 99.7s` Particles don't expand when heated.
- `102.1s` They just speed up.

## S5 — Pressure (105–125 s, 20.6 s)

- `105.4s` Gas particles keep crashing into the walls of their container.
- `108.9s` Watch the walls light up with every hit.
- `111.7s` Each hit is a tiny push.
- `113.8s` Countless pushes every second add up to gas pressure.
- `117.4s` Heat the gas, and the particles hit the walls more often, and harder.
- `121.6s` More hits, and harder hits, mean higher pressure.

- `146.6s` Warm it up, the particles speed up again, and the balloon puffs back out.
